Urban Air Quality Forecasting

Spring 2025

Python XGBoost LSTM Machine Learning Time Series Tableau

Project Overview

This capstone project focuses on understanding and forecasting urban air quality by examining the interplay between meteorological conditions, traffic patterns, and pollutant levels across three major U.S. cities: New York City, Chicago, and Los Angeles. The study spans a five-year period from 2020 to 2024, incorporating high-resolution datasets sourced from environmental agencies (e.g., EPA), public transportation authorities (e.g., NYC MTA), and global weather APIs (e.g., Open-Meteo).

Our primary objective is to predict concentrations of key pollutants—PM2.5, PM10, and NO2—by leveraging both traditional machine learning techniques and advanced deep learning models. We explore how factors such as wind speed, temperature, humidity, and vehicular traffic density influence daily air quality, and how these relationships differ across cities with distinct climate zones and traffic infrastructures.

To achieve this, we preprocess and merge heterogeneous datasets, perform feature engineering, and evaluate multiple models including XGBoost, Random Forest, LSTM, and ARIMA. These models are trained on temporal slices of the data and assessed using metrics like Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE).

This integrated approach not only identifies critical environmental predictors of air pollution but also enables near-real-time forecasting that can inform public health advisories and urban planning policies.

Implementation Highlights

  • Data: Merged datasets from EPA, Open-Meteo, and NYC MTA.
  • Models: XGBoost and Random Forest for regression; LSTM and ARIMA for time-series forecasting.
  • Performance: XGBoost achieved RMSE as low as 4.35; LSTM outperformed ARIMA for sequential predictions.
  • Visualization: Built a Tableau dashboard to explore trends and model outputs interactively.

Key Results

  • XGBoost showed highest accuracy and robustness.
  • LSTM effectively modeled long-term patterns, especially for PM2.5.
  • Traffic data significantly improved predictions for NYC.
  • Wind speed and temperature were strong predictors across cities.
